Advanced strategies

The system has rules. Your imagination does not.

Experienced players learn to compress, anchor, and personalize their stories—turning memory into an evolving skill.

✦

Two players can encode the same sequence in completely different ways and still arrive at the exact same answer.

Creativity is not a side effect. It is the engine.
01Use larger peg cards

Instead of encoding 46 as 4 and 6, use a nearby anchor such as 40 or 44 and mentally track the remaining difference. This reduces the number of physical cards required.

02Reduce the number of peg cards

For a sequence such as 43, 57, 29, 84, players can build around anchors like 44, 55, 22, and 88, then remember the small adjustments through the story itself.

03Build around anchor numbers

Highly memorable values such as 33, 44, 55, 66, 77, 88, 99, and 100 can act as stable reference points for nearby numbers.

04Use familiar associations

Personal experiences, favorite objects, and familiar places make stories easier to reconstruct because the associations already matter to you.

05Create cause-and-effect stories

Make one object trigger an action that affects the next. A chain of consequences is more memorable than an unrelated list.

Questions, answered

Everything you need to know.

Clear answers about gameplay, phases, winners, and the system behind EGP.

What is Euler's Golden Pie?

EGP is a memory game and learning system designed to make memorizing long number sequences faster, easier, and more enjoyable.

What makes EGP unique?

It combines memory training, creativity, physical components, and friendly competition in one experience.

Who goes first?

The youngest player begins the game.

What is the goal?

Memorize and accurately recall increasingly longer digit sequences using images and stories.

How does Encode work?

Players observe the digits and select peg cards that convert them into visual mnemonics.

How does Build work?

Players connect those visual mnemonics into a meaningful, vivid, or imaginative story.

How does Recall work?

Players mentally replay the story and translate each visual cue back into the original sequence.

Who wins?

The player who accurately recalls the most digits while preserving the correct order.

How are ties broken?

Tied players continue to the next sequence length until one player recalls more digits correctly.

Can peg cards stay visible?

Yes. The challenge is not remembering the cards—it is building and decoding the story effectively.
